    Graph Coloring Theory & Heuristics in Scheduling:

    In DAA, Timetable Scheduling maps to the Vertex Coloring Problem: Vertices are events $E_i$. An edge $(E_i, E_j) \in E$ is added whenever two events share a non-sharable faculty, compete for the same limited room, or exceed equipment capacity. The Chromatic Number $\chi(G)$ represents the minimum number of independent time slots required to conduct all events without conflict. Welsh-Powell sorts by degree $\deg(v)$, achieving $O(V \log V + E)$, while DSATUR dynamically maximizes the saturation degree $sat(v)$ with $O(V^2)$ bound.

    Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP)

    CSP Backtracking with MRV & Forward Checking

    Models scheduling as a 3-tuple $(X, D, C)$, where variables $X = \{E_1, \dots, E_n\}$ are events, domains $D_i$ are valid tuples of $(\text{Slot}, \text{Room}, \text{Equipment}, \text{Faculty})$, and constraints $C$ enforce temporal, room, capacity, and faculty non-overlap.

    • MRV (Minimum Remaining Values): Selects the variable with the fewest legal values remaining, failing early if an unassigned variable becomes constrained.
    • Degree Heuristic: Breaks MRV ties by picking the variable involved in the most constraints with other unassigned variables.
    • Forward Checking: Prunes incompatible domain options from neighboring variables immediately upon an assignment, cutting dead search branches early.
    Graph Theory & Chromatic Coloring

    Welsh-Powell & DSATUR Graph Coloring

    Constructs an undirected conflict graph $G = (V, E)$ where vertices represent events. An edge $(u, v)$ indicates that events $u$ and $v$ cannot share the same time slot color.

    • Welsh-Powell: Sorts vertices in non-increasing order of degrees $\deg(v)$, greedily assigns color 1, then iterates for subsequent colors. Time complexity $O(V \log V + E)$.
    • DSATUR (Degree of Saturation): Dynamically chooses the vertex with the maximum number of distinct colors assigned to its neighbors. Significantly closer to the true chromatic number $\chi(G)$ with $O(V^2)$.
    Greedy Interval Scheduling

    Multi-Resource Interval Scheduling

    Greedily sorts events by constraint density (duration, equipment scarcity, attendees) and performs a single-pass scan to find the earliest conflict-free slot and best-fit venue.

    • Pros: Extremely fast $O(n \log n + n \cdot S \cdot R)$ execution.
    • Cons: Lacks backtracking; can get stuck in local optima if early greedy choices monopolize a bottleneck room needed by subsequent long events.
